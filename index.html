## Atmospheric Reality Gap

The one image you can actually see on Mortenson's homepage is the one attached here — a dusk exterior of Fiserv Forum, shot from street level with a dramatic split sky: warm orange-yellow at the horizon, cool blue-pink above, the building's warm stone cladding and green Bucks branding lit precisely against both. The light quality is controlled, the geometry is purposeful, and the frame communicates institutional scale without feeling cold. That is a strong piece of visual evidence that Mortenson builds landmark work.

The problem is what surrounds it. The site copy declares "Builder. Innovator. Partner." and promises to make your project "better than you imagined possible." That is a premium-market positioning claim. But of the 18 content images the scan confirmed on the homepage, at least 8 are rendering as identical broken blobs — every one tagged with the same alt text "Technicians Working with Nor-Cal Controls Solar Systems" — regardless of whether the underlying article is about biosecurity facilities, hospital expansion, or sports turf. The emotional signal those broken frames actually transmit: this organization does not QA its own marketing. A $500M hospital system procurement officer comparing vendors does not extend the benefit of the doubt past a blank rectangle.

The trust cost is calibrated credibility: one exceptional photograph cannot carry a page where the majority of content images fail to load.

## Visual Narrative Coherence

The fracture is systematic, not isolated. Every News & Insights card — six articles published between April and June 2026 — pulls the same broken blob URL (`blob:http://localhost/b9a31d3949b1882a09ed2f8508d538f3`). That localhost reference means images were scraped or cached from a local build environment and never replaced with production asset paths. To a buyer clicking from a search result into a $610M biosecurity facility announcement, the article opens without a single working photograph. The project looks like a placeholder, not a portfolio win.

The careers section repeats the same broken image reference. Mortenson's copy there promises "a dynamic and inclusive culture" — a claim that requires humans, spaces, and energy to land. What loads is white space. Competing ENR top-25 firms with functioning project galleries will close that candidate and that client while Mortenson's page is still trying to resolve a localhost path.

## Metadata and Conversion Gaps

The scan confirmed 2 of 18 content images are missing alt text — an 11% gap. That is not the headline number. The headline is that the alt text covering the broken blob images reads "Technicians Working with Nor-Cal Controls Solar Systems" across every single card, including the Fiserv Forum arena story, the sustainability article, and the hospital expansion piece. Search crawlers indexing those image tags are being told that Mortenson's sports and healthcare portfolio is populated entirely by solar technicians. That is active misdirection that suppresses image search relevance across your highest-margin market categories.

The meta description at 403 characters is more than double Google's display limit of roughly 160 characters — the truncated version a buyer reads in the SERP drops mid-sentence and fails to carry a CTA. The og:image is present, which protects social share previews, but it cannot compensate for a homepage where the production image pipeline is serving localhost artifacts to live visitors. Mortenson ranks #1 for its own brand name — that traffic is arriving and converting against broken visual infrastructure that the #1 ranking itself is masking.
